A map of the inquiry

Fifty-six weeks of writing that moves between scholarly work and studio practice. It is drawn as a map rather than a syllabus because the curriculum spirals instead of progressing. Eight constellations of equal dignity, thirty-five stars, and no correct order to meet them in. Tap any circle to read the idea and where it connects.

Drag to move. Scroll to zoom.

Select a star. Larger circles hold more of the inquiry's weight, and colour marks which constellation an idea belongs to. Every star connects to others, and following those connections is closer to how the writing actually moves than reading it week by week.

Why it is a map

The thesis argues that the contemplative journey is not a linear path toward a destination. It holds that the ordinary body, previously policed and wounded, can recognise itself as the primary site of the sacred. A curriculum that argued this in a numbered sequence would be contradicting itself in its own structure, so the fifty-six weeks are organised as eight constellations that the writing weaves between rather than climbs.

The working hypothesis is that by engaging in a non-linear art practice grounded in the Path to Good star chart, the ordinary body can recognise itself as the primary site of the sacred, turning the contemplative journey from a path of spiritual progress into a recursive process of recognition and somatic integration.

Intimacy with the question

The mentor for the writing is Maria Popova's The Marginalian, and what is being borrowed is a relationship rather than a style. Popova writes from inside a question she has not finished with, holding several thinkers in the same room and letting them disagree, and the reader is trusted to follow without being managed.

The register that follows from that has a few rules. The artists get space and the theory gets compressed. Where a reception is contested, the contest is named and left unresolved rather than tidied. The closing invitation is offered peer to peer rather than as instruction, and it releases the reader from obligation rather than recruiting them.

The craft standard

Every post is held to a written standard, checked before publishing rather than felt for. No bridging by geometry, which means no claim that two things occupy the same territory or sit at another end of something, because a spatial phrase asserts a relation instead of producing one. No reversal family, which covers the not this but that construction and the stripped conjunction pair that runs two sentences where one would do. Em dashes under five per thousand words. No self-description. A star to ground ratio under one in eight, so that most of what is on the page is load-bearing.

Anchors are checked character by character against the actual source. AI tools reliably produce confident, plausible and wrong citation details, and a citation carried forward from a draft without checking is the failure most likely to end up in print.

Where it lives

The markdown repository is the archive of record. Git gives dated version history, which matters for a thesis and as evidence of authorship. Publishing happens from the archive, never inside a platform, and at least one copy is kept outside GitHub.

Patreon is distribution. Its login gate keeps the writing out of the open crawl, which handles being taken about as well as anything can. Being lost is the likelier risk and the platform does nothing about it, which is what the repository is for. YouTube was considered as a writing home and rejected, since uploading grants a worldwide sublicensable licence and Google has confirmed it trains on the material.

Why the book follows for free

Because the writing already exists in a stable format, committed week by week, the annual book asks for selection and sequencing rather than new work. That is the reason a book can be completed every November by a practice that has thirty-five studio hours a month.
